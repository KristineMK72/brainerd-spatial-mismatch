Computer & Mathematical Jobs:
A Spatial Mismatch in Brainerd

Local workers in high-skill IT and math occupations face a structural accessibility gap. Specialized jobs are heavily concentrated in the Twin Cities metro, creating classic residence–workplace mismatch.

View Key Data Deep-Dive Analyses

The Core Claim

Crow Wing County (Brainerd Lakes area) exhibits a classic spatial mismatch in Computer & Mathematical occupations. A meaningful residential supply of workers (~532 people) is poorly aligned with the geographic distribution of specialized, high-skill jobs. The majority of these opportunities are concentrated in the Twin Cities metro, forcing outflows, long-distance commuting, remote work for external employers, or local underemployment.

LQ Interpretation

Both residential and workplace LQs in Crow Wing / Northwest Minnesota sit substantially below 1.0. The Twin Cities metro is the mirror image (LQ > 1.0). This concentration differential is the quantitative core of the spatial mismatch argument.

COMPARABLE

Proof Case: Fergus Falls / Willmar

Fergus Falls (Otter Tail County) and Willmar (Kandiyohi County) share Brainerd’s structural profile: small regional centers, lakes/rural hinterlands, healthcare + manufacturing + education anchors, and a thin specialized IT base. Most Comp/Math roles are support, network, or embedded rather than pure software product or data science. Both show the same residence–workplace disconnect.

Scale

Similar micropolitan footprint and distance to a larger job cluster

IT Base

Thin specialized demand; support & embedded roles dominate

Pattern

Residential talent exists while high-skill jobs concentrate elsewhere

Spatialytics implication: If the same metrics appear in Fergus Falls and Willmar, the Brainerd argument is not idiosyncratic — it is a Greater Minnesota pattern. Spatialytics becomes a repeatable diagnostic for any mid-sized Minnesota micropolitan area facing the same knowledge-occupation mismatch.
