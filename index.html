Computer & Mathematical Jobs:
A Spatial Mismatch in Brainerd

Local workers in high-skill IT and math occupations face a structural accessibility gap. Specialized jobs are heavily concentrated in the Twin Cities metro, creating classic residence–workplace mismatch.

View Key Data Metrics Toolkit

The Core Claim

Crow Wing County (Brainerd Lakes area) exhibits a classic spatial mismatch in Computer & Mathematical occupations. A meaningful residential supply of workers (~532 people) is poorly aligned with the geographic distribution of specialized, high-skill jobs. The majority of these opportunities are concentrated in the Twin Cities metro, forcing outflows, long-distance commuting, remote work for external employers, or local underemployment.

Behavioral Outcomes of the Mismatch

  • → Out-migration of talent seeking denser career networks and specialized roles
  • → Long-distance or super-commuting to the Twin Cities
  • → Remote work for non-local (often metro or national) employers while living in Brainerd
  • → Acceptance of thinner local opportunity sets or underemployment in related roles

Historical local employer feedback (difficulty filling experienced systems analysts, software developers, and technical consultants) reinforces the pattern.

Key Interpretation

High metro concentration (74.2%) + non-trivial local residential supply (532) + thin specialized local demand = classic spatial mismatch. The mismatch is skill-specific: support and network roles exist locally; advanced software, data science, and specialized analyst roles do not at scale. Remote work softens but does not eliminate the gap for roles that still benefit from dense networks, higher metro wages, or in-person collaboration.
